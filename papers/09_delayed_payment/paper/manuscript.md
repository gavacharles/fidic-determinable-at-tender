# Which "Discount Rate"? The Adequacy and Determinability of FIDIC Financing Charges for Delayed Payment in Uganda

Gavamukulya Charles

*Draft 0.1, 15 October 2026. Paper 9 of "Determinable at tender". Target: Construction Management and Economics; Journal of Legal Affairs and Dispute Resolution in Engineering and Construction (ASCE).*

## Abstract

**Introduction.** Under FIDIC Sub-Clause 14.8 the Contractor is entitled to financing charges, compounded monthly, on any amount not paid within the payment period. The charge is set at a margin over "the discount rate of the central bank in the country of the currency of payment". Late payment is endemic on Ugandan public works. The remedy's adequacy and its reference rate are rarely examined.

**Methods.**

- Financing-charge provisions are taken from three FIDIC road contracts (Projects B, D, E).
- Each is computed under the candidate Bank of Uganda reference rates, 2011–2026: the Central Bank Rate (CBR), the 91-day Treasury bill rate, and an illustrative rediscount rate.
- These are compared with commercial bank lending rates (World Bank WDI) and with payment delays reported by the Auditor General.

**Results.**

- **Contract Data.** Project B sets 1 point above "the discount rate of the Central Bank"; Projects D and E keep the MDB default of 3 points. Bank of Uganda publishes no rate of that name.
- **Gap to lending rates.** Over 2011–2018, charges based on the CBR or T-bill rate averaged 14.0–16.2%, against a lending rate of 22.6%: a shortfall of 6.5–8.6 points a year.
- **Recovery on a delayed certificate.** On a UGX 10 bn certificate paid 200 days late at 2023–26 rates, the charge recovers:
  - 47–59% of the financing cost at the 2011–18 lending rate;
  - 59–74% at an 18% lending rate;
  - up to 93% only if "discount rate" is read as the rediscount rate.
- **Delays and cost to the Employer.** The Auditor General reports delays of 94–461 days beyond a 56-day payment period on one road. UNRA paid UGX 7.3 bn (FY2019/20) and 17 bn (FY2020/21) in interest and penalties on late payments.

**Discussion.** The financing-charge clause is indeterminate (which rate?) and, under every reading, under-compensating. The Employer finances itself at below the market cost of credit, at the Contractor's expense. Contractors respond with suspension, slowdown and claims that cost more than the interest saved. The Contract Data should name the series (e.g. "BoU Central Bank Rate as published on the first business day of each month") and set the margin by reference to the lending rate, so that the charge compensates and removes the incentive to pay late.

## 1. Introduction

Late payment is the Employer's commonest breach on public works in Uganda. The Auditor General has reported it every year from 2019 to 2025: certificates paid months late, contractors claiming interest, and the interest classed as "nugatory expenditure".

FIDIC's remedy is Sub-Clause 14.8. If the Contractor does not receive payment within the period stated in 14.7, it is entitled to financing charges, compounded monthly, on the unpaid amount for the period of delay. The rate is three percentage points above the discount rate of the central bank in the country of the currency of payment (MDB 2010; similar in 1999 and 2017), unless the Contract Data says otherwise.

The provision raises two questions:

1. Which rate? Not every central bank publishes a "discount rate". Bank of Uganda publishes a policy rate (CBR), a rediscount rate and a bank rate, and Treasury bill yields are set at auction.
2. Is the charge adequate? Does it compensate a contractor that must borrow to fund the gap, or does it make late payment cheap credit for the Employer?

## 2. Data

- **Contract Data and General Conditions** (Projects B, D, E; anonymised):
  - Project B (2023, rehabilitation, MoWT/UNRA): "one percentage point above the discount rate of the Central Bank".
  - Project D (2013, MDB 2010) and Project E (2020, urban, MDB 2010 GC): "three percentage points above the discount rate of the central bank in the country of the currency of payment, or if not available, the interbank offered rate".
  - Project E's payment periods are 60 days after the Engineer receives the statement (14.7(b)(i)), 60 days after the Employer receives it (14.7(b)(ii)), and 90 days for final payment.
- **Rates:**
  - BoU CBR (monthly, from its introduction in July 2011) and 91-day Treasury bill yields (cipi-tracker macro database);
  - commercial bank lending rate (World Bank WDI FR.INR.LEND, annual, 2000–2018);
  - the rediscount rate is represented illustratively as CBR + 4 points.
- **Delays and interest (OAG annual reports):**
  - 22 IPCs (USD 23.1 m) paid 94–461 days after submission against a 56-day period (FY2018/19);
  - interest claimed of UGX 2.16 bn and USD 0.69 m on two road projects (FY2018/19);
  - UNRA interest and penalties of UGX 7.3 bn (FY2019/20) and UGX 17 bn (FY2020/21).

## 3. Methods

- **Candidate charges.** For each month from July 2011 to May 2026, the charges are:
  - B: base + 1 and D/E: base + 3;
  - with base = CBR, T-bill 91-day, or illustrative rediscount (CBR + 4).
- **Shortfall.** Annual gap to the lending rate over the overlap (2011–2018).
- **Worked case.** A UGX 10 bn certificate paid 94, 200 or 461 days late. The charge is compounded monthly at 2023–26 average rates, against the cost of funding the same amount at the lending rate (2011–18 mean 22.6%; sensitivity 18%).

## 4. Results

**The reference rate is undefined.** Neither contract names a BoU series. Over 2019–2026 the candidates differ by up to 4 points on average:

- **Project B** (+1): CBR basis 9.9% mean; T-bill basis 10.5%.
- **Projects D/E** (+3): CBR basis 11.9%; T-bill basis 12.5%; rediscount basis 15.9%.

That choice alone changes the charge on a delayed certificate by about a third.

**Every reading under-compensates.**

- Over 2011–2018, CBR- and T-bill-based charges averaged 14.0–16.2% against commercial lending at 22.6% (Fig. 1a), a mean shortfall of 6.5–8.6 points a year. Even the rediscount reading falls 2.5 points short.
- The gap narrowed briefly when the CBR spiked (2011–12, 2015–16).
- After 2019, with the CBR at 6.5–10.25%, the charges fell to about 8–13%, below plausible lending costs.

**A worked certificate.** For UGX 10 bn paid 200 days late (the middle of the Auditor General's range), the 14.8 charge at 2023–26 rates is:

| Reading | Charge | Share of financing cost recovered |
|---|---|---|
| B: CBR + 1 | UGX 608 m | 47% |
| D/E: CBR + 3 | UGX 724 m | 55% |
| D/E: rediscount + 3 | UGX 959 m | 73% |

The contractor's financing cost at 22.6% is UGX 1,306 m (Fig. 1b). At an 18% lending rate the shares are 59%, 70% and 93%. Project B's lower margin of 1 point reduces recovery by a further 8 percentage points of cost.

**Delays are long and the interest is real.** The Auditor General's reported delays (94–461 days beyond 56) and interest bills (UGX 7.3–17 bn a year) show the clause is invoked. Because the charge is below the market rate, every shilling paid late is a loan from the contractor at a subsidised rate.

## 5. Discussion

**Indeterminacy invites dispute.** With no named series, the Contractor will claim on the highest defensible rate and the Engineer will certify on the lowest. On large or long-delayed certificates the difference is material (about a third of the charge).

**Inadequacy invites late payment.** A financing charge below the Employer's own marginal cost of funds (Treasury bill yields of 10–15%) makes delaying contractors cheaper than borrowing. A charge below the contractor's cost of credit shifts financing risk to the contractor. Contractors price that risk into tenders and respond with suspension (16.1) and slowdowns that cost far more: the Auditor General reports commitment fees of UGX 10.2 bn in one year on two delayed projects.

**A determinable clause.** Contract Data should:

1. **Name the series:** e.g. "Bank of Uganda Central Bank Rate, as published on the first business day of the month in which payment fell due". Add a fallback (91-day T-bill weighted average) and a separate series for foreign-currency portions (e.g. Term SOFR for USD).
2. **Set the margin to the cost of credit:** a margin equal to the average spread of commercial lending over the CBR in the preceding year (about 9–12 points historically), or a direct reference to the BoU weighted average lending rate.
3. **Define "due" precisely:** the payment period runs from a stated event (the Engineer's certificate, or the Employer's receipt), with a deemed date if the Engineer certifies late.

## 6. Limitations

- Lending rates are national averages (WDI, ending 2018); contractor-specific credit costs vary. Recent BoU lending-rate series should replace the 2011–18 mean.
- The rediscount rate is illustrative pending the BoU series.
- Contract Data from three contracts; payment-date data are from audit reports, not certificate ledgers. Project A's IPC workbook would allow a survival analysis of delays.
